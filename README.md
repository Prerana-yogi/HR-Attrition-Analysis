📊 HR Attrition Analysis Dashboard (Power BI)

​Welcome to the updated version of the HR Attrition Analysis Dashboard! This project is built using Power BI for interactive data visualization and Power Query for robust data cleaning and transformation. The goal of this dashboard is to uncover critical insights into employee turnover patterns, workforce demographics, and key risk factors driving attrition within the organization.  
​
##Tech Stack & Methodology

​Data Cleaning & Transformation: Power Query (M Language)
​Data Visualization & BI: Power BI Desktop
​Version Control: Git & GitHub

​🧹 Power Query Data Cleaning Steps

​Before building the dashboard, raw HR data went through rigorous preprocessing and cleaning steps in Power Query, which included:
​Handling Missing Values: Checked for nulls and blanks across critical employee columns and handled them appropriately.
​Data Type Optimization: Ensured correct data types (e.g., whole numbers for employee counts, text for departments and education fields, and decimals for rates/salaries).
​Conditional Columns & Grouping: Created custom conditional bins/groups such as Monthly Income Groups and Age Groups to segment the workforce effectively.
​
📈 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

​The updated dashboard highlights the following core metrics:
​Total Employees Left: 237 employees

​🔍 Key Insights & Findings

​Employees Left by Monthly Income Group: The highest concentration of employee attrition happens in lower income brackets, particularly under the 5K mark.
​Employees by Education Field: Attrition is heavily concentrated among employees belonging to Life Sciences and Medical educational backgrounds.
​Overtime Impact: Employees working overtime are ~3X as likely to leave compared to those who do not (30.53% attrition rate for overtime workers vs. 10.44% for non-overtime workers).
​Department Breakdown: The Research & Development department experiences the highest volume of departures (133 employees), followed by Sales (92 employees) and Human Resources (12 employees).
​Age Group Trends: The majority of employees leaving fall within the 30–39 and 18–29 age brackets.
